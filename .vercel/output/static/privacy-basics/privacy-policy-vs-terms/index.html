Privacy Policy vs Terms of Service

Understanding the key differences between these two essential legal documents.

8 min read • Updated April 2026

What Is a Privacy Policy?

A privacy policy focuses exclusively on how a company collects, uses, stores, and shares your personal information. It's about your data and your rights over it.

Key topics covered:

  • What personal information is collected
  • How data is used and processed
  • Third-party data sharing
  • Cookie and tracking technologies
  • Data retention periods
  • Your privacy rights (access, deletion, etc.)

What Are Terms of Service?

Terms of Service (also called Terms and Conditions) govern the overall relationship between you and the company. They cover rules, obligations, and legal limitations.

Key topics covered:

  • User eligibility and account rules
  • Acceptable use policies
  • Intellectual property rights
  • Liability limitations
  • Dispute resolution procedures
  • Account suspension/termination
  • Governing law and jurisdiction

Key Differences at a Glance

Privacy Policy

  • Focus: Your data
  • Purpose: Transparency
  • Driven by: Privacy laws
  • Your benefit: Know your rights
  • Updates: When data practices change

Terms of Service

  • Focus: The service
  • Purpose: Rules and obligations
  • Driven by: Business needs
  • Your benefit: Know the rules
  • Updates:随时通知

Which One Should You Read?

Read both! But here's when each matters:

Read the Privacy Policy When:

  • You're concerned about data privacy
  • You want to know what info is collected
  • You're evaluating a service's privacy practices
  • You want to exercise your data rights

Read the Terms of Service When:

  • You're signing up for a new service
  • You need to understand your obligations
  • You're concerned about liability
  • You want to know how disputes are handled

Why Both Matter

While they serve different purposes, both documents are legally binding. By using a service, you're agreeing to both.

Companies must provide both under various laws. The privacy policy is often more regulated due to privacy laws like GDPR and CCPA.
